I am Dr. Md. Mukter Hossain, a leading researcher and  Professor of Physics at Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET), Bangladesh. I completed a PhD in Physics from the University of Yamanashi, JAPAN.

My research interest is in experimental and theoretical condensed matter Physics. During my PhD, I performed experimental work on the growth of large-diameter silicon crystals, which are promising for photovoltaic applications. Apart from this experimental work, my expertise in computational materials science, based on first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations, is exceptional. I am particularly drawn to studying the different physical properties, such as electronic, optical, mechanical, thermoelectric, and transport properties, of technologically important energy-harvesting semiconducting compounds, thermoelectric materials, and industrially important MAX and Mxene phase nanolaminates. My research mainly focused on studying physical properties based on density functional theory.

**Recently, I have studied the thermoelectric, hydrogen storage, optoelectronic, and photovoltaic properties of Perovskite and double Perovskite materials, as well as chalcopyrite and chalcogenide materials, which are promising for energy and environmental applications.** **I have** **published 100+(4550+ citations, h-index= 36, i10-index=68) articles in various reputed journals, which delve deep into the first principles of electronic band structure calculations and investigations of thermo-physical, photovoltaic, and thermoelectric properties.** **I have expertise in various codes (CASTEP, Wien2k, Boltztrap, VASP, SCAPS-1D, and Quantum-Espresso, etc.).** I also have some experience in calculating carrier concentrations (valence and conduction electrons), charge mobility, and effective mass for various semiconductor materials. My future research goals are to apply my current computational skills and further develop them to advance the current research activity and meet state–of–the–art standards. My experimental and theoretical knowledge will help me adequately understand the electronic and transport properties of various energy harvesting materials of interest.
