About

The Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) is committed to excellence in undergraduate and postgraduate education and research across a broad spectrum of civil engineering disciplines. The department also emphasizes multidisciplinary areas related to the planning, design, and management of infrastructure and the built environment.

Civil engineering plays a vital role in improving public health, safety, and quality of life. The department contributes to societal well-being through education and research focused on sustainable solutions, including the provision of safe drinking water, efficient transportation systems, resilient housing, and the mitigation of natural and human-induced hazards.

The academic programs are designed to provide a strong foundation in fundamental principles while emphasizing practical applications. The curriculum integrates modern technologies, analytical tools, and advanced methodologies, enabling students to address real-world engineering challenges. The department also promotes the effective integration of engineering, planning, and management, with due consideration of environmental sustainability and the evolving socio-economic context of the country.

In addition to teaching and research, the department actively serves industry, public, and government sectors through consultancy, laboratory testing, structural analysis and design, product standardization, certification, and professional development activities.

Civil engineering is inherently multidisciplinary, combining technical expertise with a broader understanding of societal needs. The department prepares graduates to take leadership roles in infrastructure development and environmental management, equipping them with advanced knowledge, critical thinking abilities, and the professional skills required to meet the challenges of a rapidly developing society.

The department currently has 43 faculty members, including 17 with doctoral degrees, all of whom are actively engaged in teaching, research, and professional activities. In addition to their academic responsibilities, several faculty members also contribute to the administrative functions of the university.

Vision

The vision of the Department is to be recognized as a leading civil engineering department nationally and globally through excellence in education, research, and innovation, producing competent graduates and contributing to sustainable and resilient infrastructure development.

Mission

Mission:

  • M1: To produce graduates who are capable, thoughtful, ethical citizens of the world. 
  • M2: To equip students with the knowledge, intellectual engagement, and professional skills they need to succeed. 
  • M3: To foster a commitment to lifelong learning, innovation, and excellence.

    opportunities. 
     

Program Educational Objectives (PEOs)

  • PEO-1 : Knowledgeable and technically competent in civil engineering discipline in line with the industry requirement. (M2)
  • PEO-2 : Effective in communication and demonstrate good leadership quality in an organization. (M1)
  • PEO-3 : Capable of solving civil engineering problems innovatively, creatively and ethically in line with sustainable approach. (M4)
  • PEO-4 : Able to demonstrate entrepreneurship skills and recognize the need for long-life learning for successful career advancement. (M3)

 

Program Outcomes (POs)

  • PO (a): Apply knowledge of mathematics, natural science, engineering fundamentals and an engineering specialization as specified in K1 to K4 respectively to the solution of complex engineering problems.
  • PO (b): Identify, formulate, research literature and analyse complex engineering problems reaching substantiated conclusions using first principles of mathematics, natural sciences and engineering sciences. (K1 to K4)
  • PO (c): Design solutions for complex engineering problems and design systems, components or processes that meet specified needs with appropriate consideration for public health and safety, cultural, societal, and environmental considerations. (K5)
  • PO (d): Conduct investigations of complex problems using research-based knowledge (K8) and research methods including design of experiments, analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of information to provide valid conclusions.
  • PO (e): Create, select and apply appropriate techniques, resources, and modern engineering and IT tools, including prediction and modelling, to complex engineering problems, with an understanding of the limitations. (K6)
  • PO (f): Apply reasoning informed by contextual knowledge to assess societal, health, safety, legal and cultural issues and the consequent responsibilities relevant to professional engineering practice and solutions to complex engineering problems. (K7)
  • PO (g): Understand and evaluate the sustainability and impact of professional engineering work in the solution of complex engineering problems in societal and environmental contexts. (K7)
  • PO (h): Apply ethical principles and commit to professional ethics and responsibilities and norms of engineering practice. (K7)
  • PO (i): Function effectively as an individual, and as a member or leader in diverse teams and in multi-disciplinary settings.
  • PO (j): Communicate effectively on complex engineering activities with the engineering community and with society at large, such as being able to comprehend and write effective reports and design documentation, make effective presentations, and give and receive clear instructions.
  • PO (k): Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of engineering management principles and economic decision-making and apply these to one’s own work, as a member and leader in a team, to manage projects and in multidisciplinary environments.
  • PO (l): Recognize the need for, and have the preparation and ability to engage in independent and life-long learning in the broadest context of technological change.

 

Knowledge Profile

  • K1 : A systematic, theory-based understanding of the natural sciences applicable to the discipline
  • K2 : Conceptually based mathematics, numerical analysis, statistics and the formal aspects of computer and information science to support analysis and modeling applicable to the discipline
  • K3 : A systematic, theory-based formulation of engineering fundamentals required in the engineering discipline
  • K4 : Engineering specialist knowledge that provides theoretical frameworks and bodies of knowledge for the accepted practice areas in the engineering discipline; much is at the forefront of the discipline
  • K5 : Knowledge that supports engineering design in a practice area
  • K6 : Knowledge of engineering practice (technology) in the practice areas in the engineering discipline
  • K7 : Comprehension of the role of engineering in society and identified issues in engineering practice in the discipline: ethics and the engineer’s professional responsibility to public safety; the impacts of engineering activity; economic, social, cultural, environmental and sustainability
  • K8 : Engagement with selected knowledge in the research literature of the discipline

Laboratories

Strength of Materials Laboratory

The Strength of Materials Laboratory of the Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) supports both teaching and research in structural engineering, solid mechanics, and construction materials. The laboratory is designed to equip students with practical knowledge and hands-on experience essential for the analysis and design of safe, efficient, and durable structures.

To meet the evolving demands of modern engineering practice, the laboratory facilitates learning in structural behavior, material properties, and advanced testing methodologies. It is integrated with the Engineering Materials Laboratory, and together they occupy approximately 5,000 square feet of floor space.

Testing Facilities

The laboratory is equipped to conduct a wide range of experiments, including:

  • Determination of general properties of concrete
  • Tests for specific gravity, unit weight, moisture content, and water absorption of coarse and fine aggregates
  • Fineness modulus (FM) of aggregates
  • Tests on brick, mortar, and cement
  • Compressive and tensile strength tests of cement mortar
  • Stress–strain behavior and mechanical properties of materials
  • Tension, shear, impact, and torsion tests on mild steel specimens
  • Slender column tests and static bending tests
  • Hardness tests of metals
  • Helical spring tests

Major Equipment

The laboratory is equipped with modern instruments, including:

  • Universal Testing Machine (UTM)
  • Digital Universal Testing Machine
  • Spring Testing Machine
  • Hardness Testing Apparatus
  • Compression (Crushing) Testing Machine
  • Torsion Testing Machine
  • Vibro Consistometer
  • Concrete Core Cutter
  • Cement Mortar Mixer
  • Brick Cutter and Capping Apparatus

Recent Developments

To enhance research and teaching capabilities, several advanced instruments have been recently added, including a digital Universal Testing Machine, a concrete core cutter, a vibro consistometer, and a cement mortar mixer.

Geotechnical Engineering Laboratory

The Geotechnical Engineering Laboratory of the Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) supports teaching and research in soil mechanics, foundation engineering, and engineering geology. The laboratory plays a vital role in developing an understanding of the behavior of soils and rocks, and their application in the design and construction of civil engineering infrastructure, resource development, and hazard mitigation.

The laboratory is closely aligned with core courses such as soil mechanics, subsurface exploration and testing, foundation engineering, earth structures, and engineering geology.

Spanning approximately 2,100 square feet, the laboratory is equipped with modern facilities to support both undergraduate instruction and postgraduate research. It provides students with hands-on experience in determining the physical and mechanical properties of soils through a wide range of standard laboratory and field tests.

Testing Facilities

The laboratory is equipped to perform:

  • Field identification tests of soils
  • Specific gravity tests
  • Atterberg limits (liquid limit, plastic limit, shrinkage limit)
  • Grain size analysis (sieve and hydrometer methods)
  • Relative density tests
  • Field density tests
  • Permeability tests
  • Compaction tests
  • Unconfined compression tests
  • Direct shear tests
  • Consolidation tests

Major Equipment

Key equipment available in the laboratory includes:

  • Consolidation Testing Machine
  • CBR Compaction Machine
  • Direct Shear Test Machine
  • Triaxial Testing Apparatus
  • Unconfined Compression Testing Machine
  • Electric Soil Mixer
  • Sample Extractor/Injector
  • Hydrometer Jar Bath
  • Water Bath

Environmental Engineering Laboratory

The Environmental Engineering Laboratory of the Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) supports teaching and research in water and wastewater engineering, environmental quality management, and pollution control. The laboratory plays a vital role in addressing contemporary environmental challenges, including water treatment, contaminant transport, and sustainable water resources management.

The laboratory is aligned with key areas such as physical, chemical, and biological treatment processes, environmental monitoring, and modeling of pollutant transport in natural and engineered systems.

Spanning approximately 2,200 square feet, the laboratory is equipped with modern instrumentation to support both undergraduate instruction and postgraduate research. It provides hands-on experience in the analysis and design of water supply and wastewater treatment systems.

Testing Facilities

The laboratory is equipped to perform a wide range of environmental analyses, including:

  • Physical, chemical, and bacteriological analysis of water and wastewater
  • pH, Dissolved Oxygen (DO), Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD), and Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD) tests
  • Determination of nitrogen, hardness, alkalinity, and conductivity
  • Microbiological (bacteriological) analysis
  • Design and analysis of water supply and wastewater treatment systems

Major Equipment

The laboratory is equipped with advanced and standard instruments, including:

  • Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometer (AAS)
  • Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analyzer
  • Research Microscopes
  • pH Meter
  • DO Meter
  • BOD Incubator
  • COD Digester
  • Conductivity Meter

Facilities for Teaching and Research

The laboratory includes functional units and setups for water and wastewater treatment processes, enabling practical demonstration and research on treatment technologies and system performance.

Transportation Engineering Laboratory

The Transportation Engineering Laboratory of the Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) supports teaching and research in transportation planning, traffic engineering, and pavement design. The laboratory provides essential knowledge and practical skills required for the planning, design, and implementation of transportation infrastructure systems.

The laboratory is aligned with coursework in transportation engineering, covering areas such as transportation planning and economics, traffic analysis, and the design of highways and other transportation facilities.

It enables students to gain hands-on experience in the design and evaluation of both bituminous and concrete pavements. The laboratory also facilitates testing of subgrade, sub-base, and base course materials, as well as bituminous mix design and roadway capacity analysis.

Testing Facilities

The laboratory is equipped to perform:

  • Tests on subgrade, sub-base, and base materials
  • Bituminous mix design and analysis
  • Pavement material characterization
  • Roadway capacity and traffic studies
  • Testing of bituminous binders and asphalt mixtures

Major Equipment

Key equipment available in the laboratory includes:

  • Penetration Testing Apparatus
  • Los Angeles Abrasion Testing Machine
  • Aggregate Impact Testing Machine
  • California Bearing Ratio (CBR) Testing Apparatus
  • Bitumen Testing Equipment
  • Ovens for material preparation and testing

Hydraulic Engineering Laboratory

The Hydraulic Engineering Laboratory of the Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) supports teaching and research in fluid mechanics, open channel flow, and hydraulic engineering. The laboratory plays a key role in developing practical understanding of the behavior of water in natural and engineered systems, as well as in the planning, design, operation, and maintenance of hydraulic structures.

Spanning approximately 2,400 square feet, the laboratory is equipped to address multidisciplinary aspects of hydraulic engineering, including river engineering, sediment transport, and water resources systems.

A major section of the laboratory is dedicated to experimental studies on open channel flow, sediment transport, wave motion, and physical modeling of rivers, estuaries, and hydraulic structures.

Testing and Experimental Facilities

The laboratory supports experiments and demonstrations in:

  • Open channel flow and flow measurement
  • Sediment transport and river behavior
  • Wave motion and hydraulic phenomena
  • Fluid properties and flow characteristics
  • Hydraulic structures and energy conversion systems

Major Equipment

Key equipment available in the laboratory includes:

  • Fluid Friction Apparatus
  • Venturimeter
  • Bernoulli’s Apparatus
  • Sluice Gate Setup
  • Surface Tension Apparatus
  • Jet Impact Apparatus
  • Flow Measurement Demonstration Rig
  • Pelton Wheel (Impulse Turbine) Models
