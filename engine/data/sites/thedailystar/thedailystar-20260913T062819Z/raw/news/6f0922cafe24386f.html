Five women, undaunted and inspirational

The Daily Star and IPDC Finance PLC honour them for making a difference
11 HOUR(s)
UPDATED 1 MIN(s)
• NEWS
Staff Correspondent
Recipients of this year’s Unsung Women Nation Builders Awards -- Ayesha Akhter, Laboni Akhter, Razia Samad Dahlia, Suria Begum, and Aleya Begum. Ayesha, who was born without hands, holds her award with her feet. Photo: Prabir Das

Five women, five extraordinary journeys rooted in the grassroots, and five stories of resolve and sacrifice were honoured last night at the eighth edition of the Unsung Women Nation Builders Awards 2026.

While some helped transform their communities, others transformed their own lives against all adversities.

Tap here to add The Daily Star as a trusted source

Jointly organised by The Daily Star and IPDC Finance PLC, the event, held at the Krishibid Institution Bangladesh auditorium in the capital, recognised the women who overcame physical disability, poverty, social taboos and systemic neglect to bring about change in their communities.

This year’s awardees were Razia Samad Dahlia, an 83-year-old former teacher from Sherpur who spent her personal wealth on establishing a diabetic hospital, sheltering street children and supporting education and sports; Aleya Begum of Jhenaidah, who turned her experience of child marriage and suicide attempts into a 14-year mission to prevent suicide; Suria Begum, a physical education teacher who has trained hundreds of rural girls in competitive sports; Laboni Akhter, a zoology graduate who pioneered scientific rodent farming for research laboratories; and Ayesha Akhter of Gaibandha, who was born without upper limbs, learned to write with her toes, earned a master’s degree and secured a teaching job.

“They are ‘Unsung’ because society has not yet given them the full recognition they deserve. By honouring these five women tonight, we feel deeply honoured ourselves…Their sacrifices and daily service to society, rising above self-interest, are not just praiseworthy -- they are exemplary.”

Mahfuz Anam,  editor and publisher of The Daily Star

Launched in 2017, the initiative has so far recognised 52 women for their tireless and often overlooked contributions to Bangladesh’s socio-economic development.

Rizwan Dawood Shams, managing director of IPDC Finance PLC, reflected on the spirit of the initiative at the event, saying, “The most powerful and inspiring stories of Bangladesh do not always emerge from grand forums; they are scattered across villages, towns, and communities.”

He added that societal change does not always begin with loud noise, but often starts with “a woman's unwavering decision not to give up".

​“Women’s empowerment is not just about creating opportunities; recognition and visibility are equally important. When one woman's story comes forward, another receives the message that ‘I can do it too, and change is possible for me as well’,” he said.

In his speech, Mahfuz Anam, editor and publisher of The Daily Star, highlighted the core vision behind the awards, saying the true strength of Bangladesh lies in the quiet courage of women working at the grassroots.

“They are ‘Unsung’ because society has not yet given them the full recognition they deserve. By honouring these five women tonight, we feel deeply honoured ourselves."

Anam paid glowing tributes to the awardees, describing their selflessness as an inspiration for the entire nation.

“Their sacrifices and daily service to society, rising above self-interest, are not just praiseworthy -- they are exemplary,” he said, praising how women like Ayesha and Aleya turned personal adversity into groundbreaking social impact.

Image
F Minor enthrals the audience with their performance at the 8th Unsung Women Nation Builders Awards 2026 at the Krishibid Institution Bangladesh auditorium in the capital yesterday. Photo: Rashed Shumon

​He called upon the audience to draw motivation from these extraordinary journeys.

Gitanjali Singh, country representative of UN Women Bangladesh, commended the resilience of the awardees.

"Real progress happens when women are given the platform to lead.... Tonight's nominees remind us that leadership is not about a title, position, or privilege. Rather, it is the ability to create change where it is needed the most."

​Emphasising the broader impact of women’s empowerment, Singh added, “When women are empowered, nations thrive. Investing in women is always the right and the smart thing to do, and Bangladesh's history must recognise their story.”

The evening also featured audio-visual presentations highlighting the awardees’ struggles and achievements, and a musical performance by the band F Minor.

Razia Samad Dahlia - Still serving at 83

Image

The first honour of the evening, a Special Mention, went to Dahlia, affectionately called “Dahlia Khalamoni” by locals in Sherpur, who, following her husband's death in 1981 used her personal wealth entirely for the people -- from establishing the Sherpur Diabetic Hospital in 1997, providing shelter for rescued street children, to organising free eye surgeries for poor and nurture young athletes in football and cricket and more.

Farah Kabir, country director of ActionAid Bangladesh, presented her with the crest.

Receiving the award, Dahlia said, “I stood by women because they are the most neglected and oppressed, but I have stood by men as well.

“If we are to make our independence meaningful, we must reduce inequality among the people of Bangladesh. We must reach out to those who have nothing... Only then will our country stand tall -- this is my hope.”

Aleya Begum: Helping people choose life

Image

Aleya, 48, from Srifoltoli village in Jhenaidah, was recognised for turning her personal tragedy into a 14-year mission to prevent suicide.

Married off at the age of 10, Aleya experienced severe domestic and mental distress and survived two suicide attempts.

For the past 14 years, she has counselled people door-to-door, helping people across 10 villages.

At the event, she said, “I teach people to love life and help bring back those who are on the verge of ending it.

“We only have one life, and ending it is not a solution. I go from village to village reminding women that we are not helpless, that we can live like men do. We know how to live with strength, courage, and dignity.”

Gitanjali Singh presented her with the award.

Suria Begum: Building women athletes

Image

Suria, a physical education teacher from Jhenaidah, was recognised for helping overcome social taboos surrounding girls’ participation in sports.

Since joining a girls’ school in 2010, she has trained hundreds of rural girls in football, cricket, hockey and kabaddi, with many going on to compete at divisional and national levels.

Shireen Huq, a founding member of Naripokkho, presented her with the crest.

Suria said, “Back in the late 1980s, I couldn't flourish as an athlete despite having potential. Out of that regret, I established myself as a physical education teacher to train and mentor female sports talent."

She trained her daughter Juairiya Ferdous, now the national cricket team’s opening batter, and several other national-level athletes, including hockey players Irene Akter Ria and Nadira Akter and kabaddi player Anzuara Ratry.

“My vision is to establish a dedicated sports academy to nurture grassroots girls and gift more national-level players to our country."

Laboni Akhter: A different kind of farming

Image

Laboni, a zoology graduate from Shariatpur, was recognised for scientific rodent farming in Bangladesh.

Shaheen Anam, executive director of Manusher Jonno Foundation, presented her with the award.

Laboni said, “If a person truly desires it, they can achieve anything. Great things always grow from humble beginnings.

“Whatever work you do, if you put your heart and mind into it, success will surely arrive at your doorstep.”

Ayesha Akhter: A life beyond her disability

Image

The final award went to Ayesha, who was born without upper limbs and overcame physical and social barriers with the support of her mother.

Teaching herself to write with her toes, Ayesha completed a master’s degree in political science from Gaibandha Government College and went on to secure a job for herself.

Ashrafun Nahar Misti, executive director of the Women With Disabilities Development Foundation, presented the crest.

“I have overcome immense struggles since childhood to come this far. I urge other vulnerable women never to consider themselves helpless or a burden to their families and society,” Ayesha said.

The ceremony concluded with a vote of thanks from Ariful Islam, chairman of IPDC Finance PLC, who expressed gratitude to the awardees and thanked the guests and organisers for their support.
